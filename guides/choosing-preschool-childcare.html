Choosing a Preschool or Childcare Program: A Parent’s Checklist

The early years shape how children learn, relate and regulate emotions. A good program feels warm, safe and busy with purposeful play. Here is how to recognize one.

By the Edu.Care Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Start with the non-negotiables

  • Licensing: confirm the program is licensed or registered with your local authority, and look up its inspection reports.
  • Safety: secure entry, safe sleep practices for infants, clean facilities, clear sick-child and emergency procedures.
  • Background checks for all staff and volunteers.
  • Adult-to-child ratios and group sizes that meet or beat local requirements. Smaller groups mean more conversation and attention for each child.

What quality looks like on a visit

  • Teachers at children’s eye level, talking with children, asking open questions.
  • Children busy and engaged; little waiting around or screen use.
  • Books, blocks, art materials and pretend-play areas children can reach themselves.
  • Daily outdoor play.
  • Calm, respectful handling of conflict and big feelings.
  • Children’s own art on the walls, not identical crafts.

Questions to ask the director

  1. What qualifications and ongoing training do teachers have?
  2. How long have the lead teachers been here? (Low turnover is a strong quality signal.)
  3. What does a typical day look like?
  4. How do you handle biting, hitting or tantrums?
  5. How will you communicate with me about my child’s day and development?
  6. How do you support children with additional needs or who are learning a new language?
  7. What are the fees, what is included, and what are the policies for holidays and absences?

Trust your observations

Visit at least twice, including once unannounced if allowed. Watch drop-off and pick-up. After enrolling, look for signs your child is settling: talking about teachers and friends, gradually easier goodbyes and new words or skills. Some adjustment difficulty is normal for the first few weeks.

Help with costs

Many governments offer childcare subsidies, tax credits or free preschool hours for some age groups. Check your local or national government’s family benefits website.
