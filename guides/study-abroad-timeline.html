Study Abroad: A Step-by-Step Planning Timeline

Studying in another country is one of the most valuable investments a student can make, and one of the most deadline-heavy. Start roughly 18 months before your intended start date.

By the Edu.Care Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

18 to 15 months before: define your goals

  • What do you want: a full degree, a semester exchange, a language program or research?
  • Which careers are you aiming for, and which countries’ qualifications are recognized there?
  • What is your realistic total budget, including living costs and travel?
  • Shortlist three to five countries by cost, language, post-study work options and program strength.

15 to 12 months before: shortlist programs and prepare for tests

Build a list of 8 to 12 programs across ambitious, target and safer options. Note entry requirements, deadlines, application fees and required tests. Many programs require English-proficiency tests such as IELTS, TOEFL, PTE or Duolingo English Test (check which each accepts), and some require the SAT, ACT, GRE or GMAT. Book test dates early so you have time to retake if needed.

12 to 9 months before: scholarships and documents

Government and university scholarships for international students often close a year or more before the program starts. Browse the international programs in our scholarship finder, and gather documents: transcripts, translations, passport, recommendation letters and a CV.

9 to 6 months before: apply

Write a statement of purpose tailored to each program. Explain why this program, why this country, and how it connects to your goals. Our guide to application essays applies here too. Submit early: some universities review applications on a rolling basis.

6 to 3 months before: offers, funding and visa

  1. Compare offers by total cost, scholarships and outcomes, not ranking alone.
  2. Accept your offer and pay any deposit.
  3. Prepare proof of funds as required by the destination country.
  4. Apply for your student visa or permit through the official government website of the destination country. Processing times vary from weeks to months.
  5. Arrange health insurance, accommodation and any required medical checks.
Avoid visa scams: only use official government portals or licensed advisers. Nobody can guarantee a visa, and governments do not request payment via gift cards or personal transfers.

3 to 0 months before: prepare to land

  • Book flights once your visa is approved.
  • Set up a way to access money abroad and understand fees.
  • Make copies of key documents, digitally and on paper.
  • Join the university’s orientation and international student groups.
  • Learn basic local phrases and customs, and plan your first week.

Budgeting: the big categories

CategoryNotes
Tuition and feesVaries hugely by country and program; some public universities charge low or no tuition
HousingUsually the largest living cost; university housing is often cheaper in year one
Food, transport, phoneStudent discounts can cut transport costs significantly
Visa, insurance, travelOne-off but substantial; plan for them early
