How to Choose a Tutor: Questions to Ask, Costs and Red Flags

The right tutor can turn a failing grade around in weeks. The wrong one burns money and confidence. Use this framework to choose well the first time.

By the Edu.Care Editorial Team · Reviewed November 2026

Start with the real goal

Write one sentence describing what success looks like in eight weeks. “Pass Algebra I with a C or better.” “Raise the SAT math score by 60 points.” “Build reading confidence before fourth grade.” The goal decides the type of tutor you need, how often you meet, and how you will know if it is working.

What drives the price

Rates vary widely by country, city, subject and level. Expect to pay more for test prep, advanced sciences, credentialed teachers and specialists in learning differences. Packages and group sessions lower the hourly cost. Ask about cancellation policies, and avoid paying for long prepaid bundles before a trial.

Ten questions to ask before hiring

  1. What is your experience with this exact subject and grade level?
  2. How will you figure out where my child is struggling in the first session?
  3. What does a typical session look like?
  4. How do you track and report progress?
  5. How do you coordinate with the classroom teacher or syllabus?
  6. What do you do when a student is unmotivated or anxious?
  7. Can you share references from families with similar goals?
  8. Have you completed a background check, and can I see it?
  9. What are your cancellation and rescheduling policies?
  10. Can we do a trial session first?

Red flags

  • Guaranteed grade or score increases.
  • Doing homework for the student rather than teaching the method.
  • No diagnostic process and no progress updates.
  • Pressure to sign long contracts on the spot.
  • Reluctance to meet in visible spaces or to allow parents to observe sessions with younger children.
Safety first: for minors, prefer sessions in shared spaces or with a parent nearby, keep online sessions in the open with cameras on, and confirm any background check yourself.

How to tell if it is working

Within three to four sessions you should see a clear diagnosis of the problem and a plan. Within six to eight weeks you should see movement in quizzes or homework, and the student should be able to explain methods without prompting. If not, change something: frequency, focus, or the tutor.
